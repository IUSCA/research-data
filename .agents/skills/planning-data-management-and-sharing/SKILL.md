# Planning data management and sharing

Verified 2026-10-04 against NIH, NSF, IU Research, IU Data Management, and
RDC pages. Sources are listed at the end.

A data management and sharing plan is required by most federal sponsors. It
also records decisions IU policy leaves to the PI. Write it to be used, not
only submitted.

## Get help first

The RDC offers free consultations of up to one hour to IU faculty on all
campuses, to draft or refine a plan. Book through
https://researchdata.iu.edu/resources/dmp-consultation/. The RDC asks for at
least three weeks before the IU routing deadline for the proposal.

Data librarians help with plans and the rest of the data lifecycle:

| Campus | Contact |
| --- | --- |
| IU Bloomington and regional campuses | IU Libraries Research Data Services, resdata@iu.edu |
| IU Indianapolis | University Library, dataserv@iu.edu |
| IU School of Medicine | Ruth Lilly Medical Library data services |

DMPTool and the SecureMyResearch Cookbook are self-service options. The RDC
page links both.

## NIH

- **Required.** The 2023 NIH Data Management and Sharing Policy applies to
  NIH-funded research that generates scientific data (NOT-OD-21-013).
- **Required.** NIH now requires a new, simpler plan format. "Effective for
  applications submitted for due dates on or after May 25, 2026, applicants
  and recipients are required to utilize the new, simpler format"
  (NOT-OD-26-046). NIH calls the format a pilot.
- The format has yes or no questions, a 300-word limitations narrative, a
  table of data types and repositories, and a genomic data sharing question.
- **Required.** Keep data "for 3 years following closeout," per NIH Grants
  Policy Statement 8.4.2, as IU's NIH guidance cites it.
- IU Research publishes element-by-element guidance for the 2026 format. It
  was last updated 2026-09-29.

**Required.** Human genomic data falls under the NIH Genomic Data Sharing
policy. Using data from NIH controlled-access repositories carries security
requirements. See `navigating-research-data-policy`.

## NSF

- **Required.** NSF 26-202, PAPPG 24-1 Supplement 2, took effect for awards
  made on or after January 22, 2026. It renames the plan a "Data Management
  and Sharing Plan (DMSP)." Plans are built in a Research.gov tool released
  April 27, 2026.
- **Required.** "Recipients are required to share all data supporting NSF
  funded publications at the time of publication" (NSF 26-202).
- Researchers "are not required to submit datasets to NSF's Public Access
  Repository," per NSF's public access page. Choose a suitable repository.

**Open item.** The text fetch of NSF 26-202 showed inserted and struck text
without markup. Check exact wording against the rendered page before quoting
it.

## Public access to publications

- **Required.** NIH: author accepted manuscripts with acceptance dates on or
  after July 1, 2025 go to PubMed Central with no embargo (NOT-OD-25-101).
- **Required.** NSF: accepted manuscripts go to the NSF Public Access
  Repository "at or before the time of publication" (NSF 26-202).
- **Open item.** In February 2026 an appropriations statement asked OSTP to
  report on repealing the 2022 OSTP public access memo. No rescission was
  found as of 2026-10-04. Agency policies above remain in force until changed.

## What every plan should settle

IU has no research data policy that requires a plan for unfunded work.
**Recommended.** Write a short plan for every project anyway, and keep it
current. It should settle:

1. **Data and classification.** What data, from where, and its DM-01
   classification. See `classifying-research-data`.
2. **Roles.** Who collects, who curates, who controls access, and who takes
   over if the PI leaves. The PI stays accountable however tasks are
   delegated.
3. **Agreements.** Every DUA, license, and consent limit that governs the
   data, with where each is filed.
4. **Storage and computing.** Systems approved for the classification. The
   research-technologies [`planning-research-computing-work`](https://github.com/IUSCA/research-technologies/tree/main/.agents/skills/planning-research-computing-work) skill helps.
5. **Sharing.** What will be shared, when, where, and under what access. See
   `sharing-research-data`.
6. **Retention and disposal.** How long, where, and who decides on disposal.
7. **Budget.** Curation time, repository fees, and storage, which NIH and
   NSF allow as costs.

The IU Guidance for the Management of Research Data, from 2019, also
recommends an operational plan for each project. The NIST Research Data
Framework (RDaF) is a voluntary reference for these choices. Its six
lifecycle stages are Envision, Plan, Generate/Acquire, Process/Analyze,
Share/Use/Reuse, and Preserve/Discard.

## Retention

Several minimums apply. Keep data for the longest one that applies.

| Source | Minimum | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| HRPP Research Data Management policy, human subjects research | 3 years after the final expenditure report or IRB closure, whichever is longer | **Required** |
| Same policy, HIPAA authorizations | 6 years | **Required** |
| NIH Grants Policy Statement 8.4.2 | 3 years after closeout | **Required** for NIH awards |
| IU Guidance for the Management of Research Data (2019) | 5 years after completion, final financial report, or final product, whichever is longer | Guidance |
| Data agreements and sponsor terms | As stated | **Required** when they apply |

The HRPP policy adds that intellectual property, misconduct allegations,
student research, or a contract can require longer.

**Recommended.** Keep data supporting a publication for at least five years
after publication. That follows IU's 2019 guidance. Peer universities set
six or seven years.

## Keep this file current

- Watch NIH Guide notices for changes to the DMS plan pilot.
- Re-read the IU Research NIH guidance page each review. Note its updated
  date.
- **Open item.** Whether to state the HRPP's 3-year minimum or the 2019
  guidance's 5 years for research beyond human subjects. This skill states
  both, and recommends the longer one.
