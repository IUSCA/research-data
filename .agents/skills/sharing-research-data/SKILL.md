# Sharing research data

Verified 2026-10-04 against IU policy DM-02, IU Research, IU Data
Management, and RDC pages. Sources are listed at the end.

Sharing research data at IU is usually an institutional act, not a personal
one. Find out what the data's agreements, consent, and classification allow
before promising anything to a collaborator or a journal.

## Rules that always apply

- **Required.** Researchers may not sign data agreements for IU. "At IU, all
  DUAs must be reviewed and executed by designated institutional officials,
  not individual faculty or researchers," per the RDC FAQ. The Office of
  Research Administration (ORA) reviews, negotiates, and signs them.
- **Required.** Submit agreements through ORA's Unfunded Agreement Request
  Form, also called the ORA Contract Request Form. It covers data use and
  transfer, and multi-site data collection. It needs an IU login.
- **Required.** DM-02 governs giving institutional data to third parties.
  University-internal and Restricted data need the Data Steward's advice, and
  an agreement where appropriate. Critical data needs a data security review,
  made up of a questionnaire, UISO review, and Data Steward approval.
- **Required.** Per IU Data Management, the third-party assessment (3PA) "must be completed prior
  to sharing any institutional data with a third party." Contact
  iudata@iu.edu.
- **Required.** Data received under an agreement may be shared only as that
  agreement allows. This holds even after de-identification, if the agreement
  says so.
- **Required.** Human subjects data may be shared beyond the study only if
  participants consented to it, per the HRPP policies the RDC sharing guide
  cites.

**Recommended.** Contact ORA early. The RDC advises doing so "ideally before
submitting grant proposals or beginning data collection." Negotiation can
take weeks.

## Decide how to share

This follows the RDC guide "Pathways for Sharing Research Data."

**Can you share at all?** All of these must be true:

1. Every agreement on the data (DUA, DSA, collaboration agreement, or
   license) allows sharing.
2. For human participants, they consented to sharing beyond the original
   study.
3. The data is not tied to a pending patent application, a CRADA, or
   commercialization. If it is, ask the Innovation and Commercialization
   Office first.

**Can you share openly?** Only if the shared files contain no protected
elements, such as PII, PHI, or other data with legal safeguards. Otherwise,
use controlled access. The RDC guide states: "If the data contain any data
elements or information considered Personally Identifiable Information (PII)
or other protected elements, the data cannot be shared via open access
mechanisms."

**Then, two consultations, per the RDC guide:**

1. Research Contracting, about what existing agreements allow and what new
   agreement is needed.
2. A statistician, about disclosure risk and whether de-identified data stays
   usable. On the IU Bloomington campus, use the Indiana Statistical
   Consulting Center or the Biostatistics Consulting Center. At IU
   Indianapolis, use Biostatistics and Health Data Science.

## Choose where to share

| Access level | Options |
| --- | --- |
| Open | Domain repositories first; IU DataCORE (IU Bloomington); IU Indianapolis DataWorks; generalist repositories such as Zenodo, Figshare, or OSF |
| Registered or controlled | Domain controlled-access repositories, such as NIH repositories; ICPSR, which supports several access levels |
| Secure enclave | Provider-required enclaves; at IU, RADaRS for data whose custodian controls ingress and egress |

- IU publishes a generalist repository comparison matrix in IU
  ScholarWorks: https://hdl.handle.net/1805/42712.
- NIH lists supported repositories for NIH-funded data. Use one when NIH
  expects it.

**Recommended.** Prefer a repository that issues persistent identifiers,
keeps data long term, and records terms of reuse. The RDC guide points to the
NSTC and NIH "desirable characteristics" for repositories.

**Recommended.** Do not rely on publisher supplementary files or personal
cloud folders for long-term access. They are not built to keep data
findable or available.

**Recommended.** Do not give a publisher exclusive rights to research data.
Grant non-exclusive rights, so IU and the authors can still share the data.

## Outside collaborators

An IU account lets someone reach a system. It does not give them permission
to use the data on it.

- **Required.** Any access by an outside person is still sharing with a third
  party under DM-02. Agreement and assessment rules apply as above.
- **Recommended.** Put an agreement in place for an outside collaborator who
  will use IU research data, even when they work inside IU systems under an
  affiliate account. The agreement records what they may do with the data.
  The account only grants access.
- **Recommended.** When the collaborator's own institution can sign, use an
  agreement with that institution, not with the person.
- **Recommended.** Give collaborators access to the smallest set of data
  they need. Remove access when the work ends.

## Researchers joining or leaving IU

- **Required.** Human subjects data not covered by an agreement that assigns
  ownership "is the property of IU," per the HRPP Research Data Management
  policy. The PI is its custodian.
- **Required.** UA-23 says researchers "may continue to use their
  contributions to University Intellectual Property including copies of their
  Research Data... for non-commercial, academic purposes." Other limits still
  apply, such as the data's agreements, consent, and classification.
- **Recommended.** When a researcher leaves, keep the original data at IU.
  Send copies through an agreement made by ORA, such as a data transfer
  agreement with the new institution. Peer universities such as Michigan and
  Duke make this policy.
- **Recommended.** When a researcher joins IU with data from elsewhere, ask
  ORA whether an agreement is needed to bring it. The other institution may
  own it.
- **Recommended.** Before someone leaves, name the person who takes over
  custody of each dataset. Record it in the data management plan.

## International collaborators

- **Required.** Export control laws (EAR and ITAR) may limit sharing data or
  technology with foreign persons or sanctioned countries. Contact the
  Research Security Office export control team, rsohelp@iu.edu.
- **Required.** Data with personal information from people in the EU may
  fall under GDPR. Contact gdpr@iu.edu.

## Keep this file current

- Re-read the RDC sharing guide each review. It is the public form of IU's
  sharing pathways.
- **Open item.** IU has no published guidance on when an affiliate account
  is enough and when a data agreement is needed for outside collaborators.
- **Open item.** It is not published whether IU signs data agreements with
  individuals who have no institution, or what IU will share with them.
- **Open item.** It is not published whether remote access by a collaborator
  abroad counts as an international transfer, or how the collaborator's
  citizenship affects it.
- **Open item.** IU has no published procedure for a departing researcher's
  data. The recommendations above follow HRPP policy, UA-23, and peer
  policies.
